# Casino Battle — locked 2D art direction

**Status: LOCKED. Any visual deviation requires an explicit product decision.**

## The target

Casino Battle should feel like a small, lovingly illustrated fantasy world that players want to spend time in. It inherits the *visual principles* that make Slotbound appealing: clear little combatants, a restrained parchment-and-sand palette, friendly proportions, readable formations and a handmade surface. It must remain its own game with original characters, silhouettes, environments, animations and UI.

## Visual pillars

1. **Strict 2D.** Drawn sprites and 2D UI only. Use layered flat art with hand-painted texture; no 3D, pseudo-3D, 2.5D asset rendering, perspective camera, lighting simulation that reads as 3D, or isometric meshes.
2. **Warm tactility.** Light parchment, warm ivory, faded sage, clay, muted apricot, dusty blue, plum and ink. Paper/fabric grain is subtle and low contrast.
3. **Cute but not babyish.** Compact bodies, large readable heads, appealing eyes, expressive poses, soft shapes and small asymmetries. Characters feel like tiny adventurers with personality, not generic blobs.
4. **Hand-finished.** Organic contour lines, gently irregular silhouettes, restrained pencil/ink detail, visible but controlled brush texture, small hand-painted accents.
5. **Readable combat.** Separate allies and enemies with shape, pose and value contrast. Effects are short, colorful and legible; never cover the whole battlefield.
6. **Premium restraint.** Avoid neon, glossy gradients, plastic 3D shine, excessive particles, noisy UI, generic mobile-game icon packs and overdecorated frames.

## Palette tokens

| Warm shadow | `#DCCDB5` | Surface variation, card backs |
| Ink | `#4A4540` | Contours and typography |
| Sage | `#91A68A` | Nature, support, calm areas |
| Clay | `#C77F62` | Warm accents, enemy tells |

Use a compact palette per asset: one base, one shadow, one light, ink outline and at most one accent. Avoid black outlines; use warm ink.

## Shape and character language

- Units must be recognizable at small Steam Deck / laptop scale.
- Each unit has one strong silhouette cue: leaf cap, oversized scarf, shell, lantern, antlers, mushroom hood, bell, wing shape or distinctive weapon.
- Faces use a small number of marks. Eyes and posture carry emotion.
- Avoid identical body templates differentiated only by hue.
- Sprite outlines should be slightly irregular, with deliberate line-weight hierarchy.
- Equipment must not obscure the unit's face or silhouette.
- Enemies may be mischievous, odd or spooky, but not gore-heavy or grimdark.

## Battlefield and environments

- Use a side-on or top-down **flat 2D** battlefield with a clear ground plane. The prototype uses a flat horizontal arena; the final camera choice must remain 2D.
- Backgrounds are illustrated and quiet, not photo-realistic. Use a small number of layers: distant backdrop, midground props, ground plane, foreground accents.
- Keep the combat lane uncluttered; high-value silhouettes own the foreground.
- Biomes should feel cozy and distinct: mossy orchard, amber meadow, rain-kissed ruins, mushroom library, sleepy harbor, cloud garden.
- UI cards can borrow paper, stitched fabric, wood and ink textures, but must remain clean and accessible.

## Animation direction

- Idle: slow breathing, tiny sway, blinking, leaf/ear/scarf movement.
- Movement: short anticipation, soft squash-and-stretch, readable footfalls.
- Attack: clear wind-up, decisive contact frame, small hit-stop, compact impact effect.
- Damage: brief recoil and a distinct flash; never prolonged screen shake.
- Death: gentle collapse, dissolve into a few hand-drawn motes; no gore.
- Rewards: satisfying pop, soft bounce and a restrained sparkle.

## UI direction

- Parchment panels, warm ink labels, simple stitched separators, rounded corners that are slightly organic.
- Typography should be friendly and highly legible. Never use distressed fonts for small stats.
- Avoid casino motifs: no reels, roulette wheels, poker suits, chips, felt tables or slot-machine cabinets.
- The random mechanic is represented by magical sigils, an illustrated fate board, rune tiles, a charm loom or a small storybook artifact.

## Do / don't

**Do:** handmade, warm, quiet, charming, readable, character-first, tactility, strong silhouettes, small joyful details.

**Don't:** casino venue, cold UI dashboard, neon, 3D, realistic lighting, hyper-detailed clutter, generic chibi assets, glossy mobile-game style, copied Slotbound assets.

## Reference interpretation

- Slotbound is the primary reference for its muted battlefield, small unit silhouettes, hand-crafted readability and preparation-to-auto-battle rhythm. It is not a source of assets or a pixel-perfect template.
- The Swashbugglers is a useful secondary reference for its 2D cartoon adventure tone and hand-drawn character appeal. Casino Battle must keep its own softer parchment palette and original designs.
